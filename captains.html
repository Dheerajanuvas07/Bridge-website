Why your experience fits

You may already have the right instincts.

You know how to make someone feel comfortable.
You stay calm when plans change.
You communicate clearly when someone is counting on you.
You know when to help—and when something is outside your role.

Relevant backgrounds may include caregiving, social work, CNA experience, human services, community support, and related care experience.

Clear role. Clear boundaries.

This work has a clear scope.

You’re here to

  • Appointment support
  • Arrival support
  • Clinic navigation
  • Check-in assistance
  • Non-medical accompaniment
  • Agreed return support
  • Completion documentation

You’re not there to

  • Give medical advice
  • Make clinical decisions
  • Act as a healthcare provider
  • Perform unsupported lifting or personal-care tasks
  • Handle medications unless policy explicitly allows it

Common questions

Captain FAQ

Who can become a Bridge Captain?

Bridge is looking for local people with relevant experience supporting older adults, navigating community settings, and showing up reliably. Experience may come from caregiving, social work, community support, CNA work, or related human-service roles.

Do I need caregiving experience?

Not necessarily, but the role does require a calm, reliable approach and comfort supporting older adults during a medical appointment. Experience in caregiving, social work, community support, or similar roles is especially relevant.

Is this medical work?

No. Bridge is non-medical support. Captains help with navigation, check-in, accompaniment, and follow-through. Bridge does not provide medical advice or make clinical decisions.

How long is an assignment?

Assignments are built around one appointment and the agreed support period. The model is designed around a clear appointment journey rather than a broad ongoing shift.

How does transportation work?

Transportation details depend on each assignment and are confirmed before the appointment begins. The goal is to keep the role clear, safe, and dependable.

How are assignments handled?

Assignments are scheduled based on appointment details, your availability, and the level of support needed. We confirm the plan before you move forward.

What happens if an appointment runs late?

Bridge is built around a defined appointment flow. If an appointment runs longer than expected, timing changes are communicated clearly and follow the agreed support plan.

How does compensation work?

Compensation is discussed during onboarding. Bridge does not publish a rate until the model is finalized and approved for recruiting.

What happens in an emergency?

Bridge Captains follow non-medical support protocols. In a medical emergency, the appropriate action is to call 911 immediately and notify the family and Bridge point of contact.
